How good is the market's own day-ahead forecast?

The day-ahead price is settlement truth for the bill, but it is itself a forecast. Here it is measured against the price that forms at real time: the German uniform imbalance price (reBAP). German-Luxembourg zone.

 

How to read this page (30 seconds)

Day-ahead price: the auction that clears at noon the day before delivery. A dynamic tariff bills the household at exactly this price, so for the household it is not a forecast, it is the bill.

Real-time (reBAP) price: the German uniform imbalance price, the settlement price for deviating from schedule, published by the four transmission system operators. It is zone-local and can be negative; read it as what real-time scarcity cost, not as a price anyone is billed directly.

Spread: real-time minus day-ahead, in ct/kWh. A spread near zero means the day-ahead price already knew what real time would say.

Why the household verdict cannot move: the household settles at day-ahead no matter what real time does, so every euro figure here is a hypothetical for flexibility settled closer to delivery, not a household bill.

The question and the method

The day-ahead price is ground truth for the bill and a forecast for everything else. Measuring it against the price that forms at delivery turns "is the market good at predicting itself?" into a question with a number attached.

Everything else in this project treats the day-ahead price as ground truth. It is, for the bill. But that price is the outcome of an auction that clears on forecast demand and forecast wind and solar, so it embodies the market's own day-ahead forecast. How well that forecast holds up shows in the price that forms at real time, when the actual system state is known: the imbalance price.

Germany's real-time price is the reBAP, the single balancing-energy price the four grid operators settle deviations at. ENTSO-E does not publish it (a probe confirmed that), so it is fetched from netztransparenz.de and compared per delivery hour against the same year of day-ahead prices.

The imbalance price is heavy-tailed: most hours are quiet and a few scarcity hours are extreme. Those tails are the phenomenon, not noise, so the spreads below are reported as medians and inter-quartile ranges alongside means, and no outliers are trimmed.

Where the day-ahead price is systematically off

The bias is not spread evenly across the day. An unbiased forecast would scatter around zero at every hour; a consistent sign at a given hour means the day-ahead auction reliably under- or over-prices that part of the day, and those hours are where a flexibility operator would look first.

How much more variable is the real-time price?

Real time is more variable than day-ahead in every season, from about 1.6 times in summer to 2.2 times in winter. A forecast that captures the predictable shape of the day leaves the surprises for real time, so this ratio is a direct read on how much of the day the auction already knew.

Do the cheapest hours survive to real time?

This is the product question of the whole project. If the three cheapest day-ahead hours are still the three cheapest at real-time prices, picking hours on day-ahead information loses nothing when value is judged closer to delivery. If they are not, an operator exposed to real-time prices would want to re-pick.

Is that euro figure a margin or a handful of days?

A small share of days carries most of the annual figure, so the yearly number and a typical day are two different facts. An annual figure is a sum over days, and a sum is the day count times the mean, so the mean is the right input for anything annual: whoever holds the position all year accumulates it, tail days included. But the mean says nothing about an ordinary day, and on a fat-tailed series the two diverge a long way.

This compares an auction price with a settlement price, not a live trading strategy, and it cannot change what the household pays, which is the day-ahead price. It answers a narrower question, whether the day-ahead price is a good proxy for real-time scarcity, which matters for flexibility settled closer to real time.
